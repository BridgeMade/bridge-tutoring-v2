# Bridge voice — read before writing any customer-facing copy

Source of truth: Bridge Brand Guidelines 2025. This file is the working
reference for website, landing pages, blog posts, and any parent-facing copy.
Read it before writing, and re-read the "Tells that it's AI-written" section
before shipping.

---

## Who we are talking to

Almost always **the parent** (70% the mother, 35–50, professional, Sandton /
Fourways / Menlyn / Waterkloof, child in a private or Model C school). She is
already paying school fees and needs to know tutoring is *going somewhere* — not
just homework help. She wants progress she can see without micromanaging.

Sometimes the student (Grade 4–12) or a prospective tutor — but the default
reader is the parent. Always **"your child"**, never "students" or "learners"
in direct copy.

---

## Brand character — The Trusted Guide

Bridge is the calm, knowledgeable figure a parent leans on. **Not a salesperson
— a trusted advisor.** Five traits:

- **Steady** — never rushed, never reactive. Quiet confidence that things are under control.
- **Expert** — authority, not arrogance. Earns trust through substance, not credentials.
- **Personal** — knows the child's name, goals, struggles. "Your child," always.
- **Transparent** — honest about where a student is, clear about what's being done.
- **Encouraging** — finds and names progress, even small wins. Reframes difficulty as growth.

## How to write

- **Start with the answer**, then add context. Don't bury the point.
- **Speak to the parent's need for certainty.** No pressure, no urgency tactics.
- **Be specific.** "Her tutor focused on fractions" beats "we help with maths."
- **Name progress.** Reframe difficulty toward growth, never toward fear.
- Short paragraphs (1–4 sentences). Plain, clear sentences.
- **South African / UK English**: "centre", "organise", "programme", "maths",
  "marks", "Grade 12 / matric". Never US spelling.
- Refer to the company as **Bridge** or **Bridge Tutoring Services**.

## Never use

- Fear-based framing: "Don't let your child fall behind!"
- Salesy urgency: "TODAY", "now", "limited spots", ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- Exclamation marks. Emojis.
- Hype words: "unlock", "leverage", "seamless", "world-class", "supercharge",
  "game-changing", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Cliché: "take it to the next level", "passionate about education".
- Treating the parent as a lead instead of a partner.

---

## On-brand vs off-brand (from the guidelines)

**Off-brand:**
> "Don't let your child fall behind! Our tutors will MAXIMISE their potential TODAY!"
Fear-based, salesy, exclamation marks. Treats the parent as a lead.

**On-brand:**
> "Every student learns differently. We build a plan around yours — and keep you
> informed every step of the way."
Calm. Personal. Speaks to the need for certainty. No pressure.

---

## Tone by channel (character never changes, tone adapts)

- **Website** — warm and confident, avoids cliché. "Every student learns differently. We find what works for yours."
- **Social** — punchy, no exclamations. "Most tutors teach the work. We teach your child how to learn it. There's a difference."
- **WhatsApp / updates** — specific, personal, brief. "Aisha had a great session today. Her tutor focused on fractions — here's what's planned next."
- **Email (first contact)** — professional, unhurried. "Before we match your child with a tutor, we'd like to understand where they are."

---

## Tells that it's AI-written — delete before shipping

- Any banned word above (especially "unlock", "seamless", "world-class").
- Exclamation marks or emoji.
- Generic openers: "In today's world…", "When it comes to tutoring…".
- Hedging filler: "It's important to note that", "Whether you're X or Y".
- Three-adjective lists ("expert, dedicated, passionate tutors").
- "Students/learners" where it should be "your child".
- Urgency or fear. Bridge is steady — if it reads anxious, rewrite it.
- Rounded or invented numbers — use only the figures in [[facts]].
